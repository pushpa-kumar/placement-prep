Study priority

  • Core DSA first. Just over half of everything in this dataset is a straight algorithms/data-structures problem — this is the highest-leverage prep and overlaps directly with this site's CP/DSA Guide.
  • Quant/probability puzzles second if targeting prop-trading firms specifically — see the important caveat below, because this bucket actually contains two very different things.
  • MCQ/aptitude/SQL sections are a real gatekeeping stage at some companies even though they carry little algorithmic depth — don't skip practicing them just because they look "easy."
Important caveat on the "Quant/Probability" bucket: at corporate/banking OAs (American Express, NatWest) this label mostly covers numerical-reasoning aptitude questions ("which number is missing: 3, 5, 9, 15, __, 33?") — test-taking speed and pattern-spotting, not real math. At the prop-trading firms (Ebullient Securities, Graviton Research Capital, IMC Trading, Squarepoint Capital, Plutus Research) the same label covers genuine probability/statistics/combinatorics brainteasers (hypothesis testing, expected-value puzzles, coin-toss probability, combinatorial proofs) — the kind of quant interview prep this site is actually built around. Don't assume a company is "quant-heavy" from the topic tag alone; check which cluster it's in using the table below.

Reading the clusters

  • Prop-trading / quant cluster (Ebullient Securities, Squarepoint Capital, Graviton Research Capital, IMC Trading, Plutus Research, AlgoQuant): hard Codeforces/AtCoder-difficulty DSA plus genuine probability/expectation/combinatorics puzzles. This cluster looks the most like the rest of this site's HFT-focused content, and is where a Codeforces/CSES practice habit pays off most directly.
  • American Express: dominated by numerical-reasoning aptitude MCQs and Python/SQL sections rather than hard DSA — closer to a screening filter than a coding test. Low practice-link coverage means most of it can't be drilled on a public judge; timed mental-math and SQL-query practice matters more here.
  • NatWest: mostly hands-on custom array/string/graph coding problems and logic/constraint puzzles, with far fewer MCQs than Amex.
  • Mid-tier product/bank OAs (Wells Fargo, Oracle, Deutsche Bank, Meesho, Microsoft, Rakuten, Media.net): standard LeetCode-medium-equivalent DSA sets, the closest thing to "generic" interview prep in this dataset.
  • Gap this surfaced: Convex Hull / computational geometry (LeetCode "Erect the Fence" asked independently at two companies, plus "Rectangle Area II") had zero coverage in the CP/DSA Guide's original 14 topics — a new Computational Geometry page was added to close that gap.
